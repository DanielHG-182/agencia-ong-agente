## 2.1 The Problem TRUSTLABS Addresses

Skills are key drivers of employability and economic mobility. They open the door
to new opportunities — yet large segments of the adult population lack the motivation
to engage in learning activities, even when opportunities are available and accessible.
This motivational deficit is not a personal failing: it is a structural outcome of
earlier negative experiences in formal educational settings that have created a lasting
aversion to organised learning. For many adults, particularly those with low basic
skills, school was a place of failure, not growth. That association does not disappear
with age.

The challenge is compounded by structural barriers that make engagement practically
difficult even for those adults who are motivated. Time constraints arising from
work schedules, care responsibilities for children or elderly relatives, geographic
distance from provision, and the absence of flexible learning formats all create
friction that discourages participation. These barriers do not operate in isolation —
they interact and reinforce each other, making the path back into learning feel
prohibitively complex.

This problem has been consistently documented across European contexts. The urgency
is acute: people entering the labour market today will spend approximately four
decades working, during which the skills demanded of them will change fundamentally
multiple times. The capacity to continue learning throughout adult life is not
optional — it is a precondition for economic participation and social inclusion.
Yet the adults most in need of upskilling are precisely those least likely to engage
with available provision. Reaching them requires a fundamentally different approach.

## 2.2 The TRUSTLABS Response

TRUSTLABS pilots innovative, evidence-based approaches to stimulate adult engagement
in learning by meeting adults where they already are — in their usual and trusted
environments. Rather than expecting disengaged adults to enter formal or semi-formal
educational settings that they have already rejected, TRUSTLABS brings learning
opportunities to the spaces where adults feel safe, comfortable and in control.

These environments include libraries, community centres, cultural facilities, sport
and leisure centres, health and social inclusion services, workplaces, and public
employment services. The choice of environment is not incidental — it is the
methodological core of the project. Physical and social familiarity reduces the
psychological barrier to engagement. When learning happens in a space that an adult
already trusts and frequents, the decision to participate does not require overcoming
the identity-level resistance that formal education settings often trigger.

The learning offer delivered through TRUSTLABS is non-stigmatising, tailored,
flexible and quality-assured. Non-stigmatising means that participation does not
require adults to identify as lacking skills or as needing remediation — framing
that consistently deters the target group. Tailored means that content and format
respond to the specific needs, life circumstances and prior experiences of the
learner. Flexible means that schedules, formats and pacing accommodate time
constraints and care responsibilities rather than demanding that adults adapt to
institutional rhythms. Quality-assured means that the learning experience meets
rigorous standards regardless of where or how it is delivered.

TRUSTLABS integrates skills assessment, validation and career guidance into the
learning offer in a user-friendly, accessible way — enabling learners to understand
what they have acquired, have it recognised, and connect it to their employment
and life trajectories.

## 2.3 Partnership Architecture

TRUSTLABS is built on the principle that no single type of organisation can reach
and serve disengaged adults alone. The multi-actor partnership at the heart of
the project brings together organisations with complementary mandates, relationships
and capacities: learning providers who understand pedagogy and curriculum; community
organisations who have established trust with hard-to-reach populations; public
employment services and social services who interact with adults at moments of
economic and personal transition; policy bodies who can translate project learning
into systemic change; and research institutions who can generate and validate evidence.

Partnership approaches of this kind are demonstrably more effective than single-actor
interventions. They create the referral pathways, shared accountability structures and
combined reach that individual organisations cannot generate alone. TRUSTLABS develops
these partnerships not just for the duration of the project but as lasting infrastructure
— creating models of cooperation that outlive the funding and continue to serve adults
in the participating communities.

Intergenerational approaches are explored within the partnership framework where
contextually appropriate, recognising that mixed-age learning environments can reduce
stigma, build social cohesion and create mutual benefit between generations.

## 2.4 Policy Alignment and Expected Contribution

TRUSTLABS is designed to contribute measurably to the EU headline target of 60%
adult participation in learning by 2030. This target represents a collective
European commitment to building learning societies in which adults at all skill
levels and life stages can access and benefit from ongoing education and training.
TRUSTLABS addresses the specific population segment — low-skilled, disengaged adults —
whose exclusion is the primary reason the 60% target remains distant in most
Member States.

The project generates five categories of output that feed directly into this policy
objective. First, it develops systemic approaches to strengthening engagement of
the target group in trusted environments that can be adopted and adapted by learning
providers across Member States. Second, it produces evidence of skills and competences
acquired by participants that demonstrates the effectiveness of the approach. Third,
it develops delivery models, guidance materials, frameworks and tools that increase
the capacity of educators, trainers and learning providers to serve this population.
Fourth, it establishes lasting partnership models that create durable infrastructure
for innovative adult learning provision. Fifth, it produces models for upscaling
successful approaches across different national, regional and local contexts —
enabling Member States to learn from each other and accelerate the pace of change.

TRUSTLABS does not treat policy contribution as an afterthought. Evidence generation,
documentation of transferable models, and active dissemination to policy audiences
are built into the project design from the outset, ensuring that what works in
TRUSTLABS pilots does not remain confined to the partner organisations but informs
practice and policy at the national and European level.
